What Is Gut Health?

Guts, which include the stomach and intestines, are the powerhouse of digestion in the body responsible for absorption of nutrients, immunity, and to elimination of waste. Your gut is home to trillions of microorganisms- bacteria, viruses, and fungi- known as the gut microbiome. A diverse number of microbiomes is key to good gut health, supporting strong immunity, reducing inflammation, and protecting against chronic diseases. However, an imbalanced gut, often caused by poor diet, stress, or antibiotics, can lead to digestive issues, weakened immunity, weight fluctuation, and even mental health issues like anxiety and depression. Signs Of An Unhealthy Gut

Your gut health directly impacts your overall well-being, and when it's out of balance, your body sends clear warning signs. Watch out for these signs of unhealthy gut:

  • Having digestive discomfort, regular bloating, constipation, diarrhoea, heartburn, acid reflux, or undigested food in stool may indicate poor gut health.
  • Unexplained weight fluctuations or sudden weight changes, unintentional weight loss, or persistent sugar craving may signal gut imbalance, such as harmful bacteria affecting fat storage, insulin resistance, malabsorption, or disruption in appetite-regulating hormones.
  • Chronic fatigue, poor sleep, or brain fog may stem from gut imbalances, including disrupted serotonin and melatonin production, gut inflammation, or toxin leakage in the blood.
  • Skin issues like eczema, acne, or dull skin can also be caused by poor gut health, like a leaky gut, dysbiosis, or poor nutrient absorption.
  • Sudden sensitivities or new food intolerances can suggest damaged gut health, gluten/ dairy reactions may stem from bacterial imbalance, while histamine intolerance may indicate enzyme deficiency.
  • Mental health can also be impacted by gut health. Anxiety, depression, ADHD, or irritability can worsen due to gut inflammation and imbalanced neurotransmitters.

Myths about gut health

Myth 1 - All bacteria are bad.

Fact- Our gut needs beneficial bacteria for digestion, immunity, and even mental health. Only harmful strains cause disease.

Myth 2- probiotics work the same for everyone.

Fact- Effectiveness depends upon the individual's gut microbiome. Strains that may help one individual may not help the other.

Myth 3- gut health only affects digestion.

Fact- Research links gut imbalances to mood disorders, skin conditions, autoimmune diseases, and chronic inflammation.
